The three problems

Month read first. The shared parser reads every date month first. Our tenants write the day first, so any date with a day of 12 or less comes out flipped: 5 June becomes 6 May. These reports name both dates, and the day and month are swapped every time. A few name no date at all (“wrong day”, “a setting for my country writes the day first”); they come from the same tenants and channels, so they count here.

MM/DD hint on booking-v3. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Its three tenants write the day first, so their people type the month first, as the hint asks, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. The reports read exactly like the parser’s, but the fix is in the form, so the parser fix does not close them.

Moved by one day. These dates are not flipped; they move by a day: Tuesday shows as Monday, one city gets a different day than another, an invite is stamped before its booking was made. That points at time zones, not at date order. The desk marked them when it read them (desk-notes.json), then filed them by tenant, under the parser.
